## The full picture: cost of living, geography, and industry mix

Median household income varies by nearly **$53,000** between the
highest-ranked and lowest-ranked state — a gap driven by cost of living,
industry mix, and geography, not just "who works harder." A $70,000 income
in rural Mississippi and a $70,000 income in the DC suburbs buy very
different lives.

All figures below are median household income from the Census Bureau's ACS
2020–2024 5-Year Estimates (table B19013) — the same data every state page
on this site uses.

## What this ranking doesn't tell you

A high rank doesn't automatically mean "better off" — it doesn't account for
state or local taxes, or how far that income actually goes locally. A
household earning the median in a high-ranked state can still feel squeezed
once rent and childcare are priced in.

For the exact number and national percentile for any specific state, visit
its own page — every one of the 50 states (plus D.C.) has its own median
income, top-earner thresholds, and county-by-county breakdown.
