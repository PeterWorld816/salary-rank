## The numbers, by age

These are household net worth figures (total assets minus debt) from the
Federal Reserve's 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF) — the same
government survey behind every net-worth percentile on this site.

| Age | Median net worth | Average net worth |
|---|---|---|
| Under 35 | $39,000 | $183,500 |
| 35–44 | $135,600 | $549,600 |
| 45–54 | $247,200 | $975,800 |
| 55–64 | $364,500 | $1,566,900 |
| 65+ | $372,750 | $1,709,350 |

Overall, across every age combined, the median US household net worth is
**$192,084** and the average is **$1,059,470**.

## Why "median" and "average" are so far apart here

Look at the under-35 row: the average ($183,500) is more than **4.7 times**
the median ($39,000). That gap isn't a typo — a relatively small number of
very wealthy households (often with home equity, business ownership, or
inherited wealth) pull the average up dramatically, while the typical
household in that bracket has far less.

The median — the midpoint, where half of households have more and half have
less — is the more honest answer to "what's normal for my age," which is why
it's worth quoting over the average whenever you can.

## The under-35 caveat

The Fed's own published brackets start at "under 35," not "under 25" — so
this site's "Under 25" and "25–34" age bands both use the same under-35
figures above. There isn't a separate published number for the youngest
slice of that group; it's folded into the whole under-35 bracket.

## What "net worth" includes (and doesn't)

Net worth here means total assets — cash, retirement accounts, home equity,
investments — minus total debt. It's a broader number than a 401(k) balance
alone (see your own [401(k) comparison](/us) for that specific figure) or
take-home income; someone can have a modest salary and a large net worth
built up over decades, or the reverse.
